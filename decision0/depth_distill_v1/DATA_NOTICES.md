# Decision-0 across-depth pilot: data and provenance

`snli_replay.json` is a selected subset of SNLI 1.0 by Samuel R. Bowman, Gabor Angeli, Christopher Potts, and Christopher D. Manning (2015), *A large annotated corpus for learning natural language inference*. Source: https://nlp.stanford.edu/projects/snli/ . SNLI is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/ . These selected texts, labels, and their presentation adaptations remain under that license. No endorsement is implied.

Changes: selected 32 original training pairs and 24 original test pairs by fixed class quotas and SHA-256-sorted pair IDs, with at most one claim per premise; retained the archived upstream wording and labels; added model-request wrappers and shuffled outcome order. The original pair IDs and split provenance remain included. Source texts were recovered from an earlier research archive, so the 24 test pairs are external regression examples, not a newly untouched final holdout. Ambiguous or awkward upstream wording was not silently relabeled.

The other scenarios are independently constructed synthetic decision examples. A training row is not an independent source situation: the 64 synthetic training rows derive from 32 paired situations. The 96 synthetic held-out rows derive from 48 paired situations. Canonical duplicate checks ignore cosmetic holdout prefixes and outcome order. They do not prove absence of shared template bias, semantic resemblance, or pretraining exposure.

Protocol v2 is obtained by applying `revision2.patch` to the committed v1 source. This patch and the replay file are frozen before new teacher outputs, training, or benchmark selection. The superseded v1 run was cancelled before model execution. The training budget is one pass through 96 requests, 24 optimizer updates per arm and seed, and rank-eight adapters at all 32 feed-forward down-projections. This is a small method pilot, not a release-ready model or a demonstration of long-context learning.

The public JevBench corpus remains evaluation-only and repeatedly inspected regression evidence. There is no hidden-test, official-score, production-speed, or accepted-cost claim.
